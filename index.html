Knowledge Base

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Simple Interest and how is it calculated?

Simple interest is calculated solely on the original principal amount. The formula is I = (P × R × T) / 100, where P is the principal amount, R is the annual interest rate, and T is the duration in years. Unlike compound interest, interest accrued in prior periods does not earn interest.

How does Simple Interest differ from Compound Interest?

In Simple Interest, the interest earned each year remains constant because it is always computed on the initial principal. In Compound Interest, interest is added to the principal periodically ("interest on interest"), causing the balance to grow exponentially over time.

Where is Simple Interest commonly used in the real world?

Simple interest is frequently applied in short-term personal loans, car loans, fixed-income government bonds, promissory notes, and certain certificates of deposit (CDs).
